Block hidden trackers

⏱ 8 minutes 📱 iPhone and Android 🔧 One free app install

Free apps are often funded by tracking rather than ads alone. A DNS-level blocker stops most of it in one setting, without touching each app individually.

What's actually happening

Many free apps include small pieces of code from advertising and analytics companies. Every time you open the app, that code can quietly report your activity back to a data broker — separate from anything the app itself does.

DNS blocking works by refusing to connect to known tracker domains at the network level, before the request even leaves your device. It doesn't require touching individual app settings.

Setting it up

  1. Install a reputable free DNS blocker — NextDNS and AdGuard DNS are widely used, open about their method, and free for personal use
  2. On iPhone: Settings → General → VPN & Device Management → DNS, and select the provider's configuration
  3. On Android: Settings → Network & Internet → Private DNS, and enter the provider's hostname
  4. Restart your phone once to apply it fully
A DNS blocker won't work while connected to a VPN unless the VPN app specifically supports it — the two features can conflict. Check your VPN's settings if you use one.

How to tell it's working

Most tracker-blocking DNS providers offer a dashboard showing blocked requests in real time. After setup, open a few apps you use daily and check the dashboard — you'll typically see dozens of blocked tracker connections within the first few minutes.

What this doesn't cover

  • Tracking built directly into the app's own servers (not third-party) — DNS blocking only stops external tracker domains
  • Browser-based tracking while using Safari or Chrome — a separate browser extension like uBlock Origin (on desktop) handles that layer
