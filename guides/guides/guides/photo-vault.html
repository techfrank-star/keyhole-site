Isolate sensitive files from your everyday photo roll

⏱ 7 minutes 📱 iPhone and Android 🔧 Built-in tools only

IDs, screenshots of documents, and financial info often end up mixed in with vacation photos and memes. A separate, locked space keeps them apart from anything you might casually share or show someone.

Why this is worth doing

The risk isn't usually a hacker — it's handing your phone to a friend to show them a photo, and a screenshot of your passport sitting three photos away in the same roll. Separating sensitive files removes that everyday exposure.

On iPhone: use the built-in Hidden album, then go further

  1. Open Photos, select the sensitive image, tap the share icon → Hide
  2. This moves it to Albums → Hidden, which is not shown in your main camera roll
  3. For real protection, go to Settings → Photos and ensure Hidden Album requires Face ID to view
  4. For documents specifically, consider using the Notes app's locked notes feature instead — photos attached to a locked note require Face ID individually
The Hidden album on iPhone is better than nothing, but it's still discoverable by anyone who knows where to look. For genuinely sensitive documents (passport, ID, bank details), a locked note or a dedicated vault app offers stronger protection.

On Android: use a Secure Folder or Private Space

  1. Samsung devices: Settings → Security and privacy → Secure Folder — this creates an encrypted, separate storage area with its own lock
  2. Other Android devices: newer versions include Settings → Security → Private Space, which works similarly
  3. Move sensitive photos and files into this space; they won't appear in your main gallery at all
  4. Set a separate PIN or biometric lock for the secure space, different from your main phone lock if possible

What to actually move

  • Photos or scans of IDs, passports, or driver's licenses
  • Screenshots of bank details, card numbers, or statements
  • Medical documents or prescriptions
  • Any screenshot containing a password or recovery code
Don't rely on a "hidden" folder as your only backup of critical documents. If your phone is lost, damaged, or reset, hidden files can be lost too — keep a separate encrypted backup (like an encrypted note synced to your password manager) for anything irreplaceable.
