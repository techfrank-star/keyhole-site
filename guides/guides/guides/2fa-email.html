Secure your email with two-factor authentication

⏱ 5 minutes 📱 iPhone and Android 🔧 No new app strictly required

Your email is the recovery path for almost every other account you own. If someone gets into it, they can reset passwords everywhere else too — this is the single highest-impact fix on this whole site.

What 2FA actually does

Two-factor authentication means logging in requires your password and a second proof — usually a code from your phone. Even if someone steals or guesses your password, they're stopped at that second step.

Skip SMS codes if you can. Text-message codes can be intercepted through a SIM-swap attack. An authenticator app is meaningfully stronger and just as easy to use.

Setting it up on Gmail

  1. Go to myaccount.google.com/security
  2. Tap 2-Step Verification → Get started
  3. Choose Authenticator app instead of text message when offered
  4. Install Google Authenticator or Authy (both free), then scan the QR code shown
  5. Enter the 6-digit code the app generates to confirm

Setting it up on Outlook / Microsoft accounts

  1. Go to account.microsoft.com/security
  2. Select Advanced security options
  3. Turn on Two-step verification and choose the authenticator app option

Setting it up on Yahoo

  1. Go to Account Info → Account Security
  2. Toggle on Two-step verification
  3. Select the authenticator app method when prompted
Save your backup codes. Every provider gives you one-time backup codes when you set this up — write them down somewhere physical. Losing your phone without these means being locked out of your own account.

What this protects you from

  • Password reuse breaches — even with a leaked password, the attacker still needs your phone
  • Phishing attempts that trick you into typing your password on a fake site
  • Anyone guessing a weak password you may have set years ago
